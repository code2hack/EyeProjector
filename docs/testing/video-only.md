# Video and control without forwarded audio

Worker: Worker-EyeProjector-7 · issue [#7](https://github.com/code2hack/EyeProjector/issues/7)

The Phone starts pinned scrcpy 5.0.1 with `audio=false`, `video=true`, `control=true`, `send_stream_meta=true` and `send_frame_meta=true`. Reverse Unix socket acceptance is video then control. The video stream begins with the four-byte H.264 codec ID, followed by the existing session/geometry records and encoded packets. No audio socket or audio codec header is read. This matches upstream [DesktopConnection](https://github.com/Genymobile/scrcpy/blob/a60891aea193d92e7e5c3942700eca63f9d19a5f/server/src/main/java/com/genymobile/scrcpy/device/DesktopConnection.java) and [Streamer](https://github.com/Genymobile/scrcpy/blob/a60891aea193d92e7e5c3942700eca63f9d19a5f/server/src/main/java/com/genymobile/scrcpy/device/Streamer.java).

Wire revision 3 keeps the native control channel IDs and requires H.264. Its welcome has no audio-required flag, and retired audio channel IDs 2 and 7 are rejected. Both endpoints must update together; revisions 1 and 2 are incompatible. Mutual TLS, peer trust, framing bounds and geometry bounds retain their existing requirements.

Each Surface decoder maps the first non-config packet's video PTS to its local monotonic packet-receipt time. Later frames preserve source PTS intervals. Reconnect and geometry replacement allocate a new decoder and timing origin. Config packets do not start that clock. The existing 16-frame tracking bounds, 100 ms late-frame drop and 500 ms presentation lookahead remain. Decoder input uses 50 ms availability polls, with the progress-aware retry described below. STREAMING requires a render callback from the current video decoder; it has no audio or Bluetooth readiness dependency. Static UI needs no periodic audio-clock updates. Startup and writes remain bounded; elapsed active-session time does not cause expiry.

Status identifies audio as `system-bluetooth`, which describes the selected system-managed path and does not claim a Bluetooth connection or audible playback. Audio focus, playback, recording and Bluetooth routing are outside the projection transport. There is no Receiver AudioTrack or forwarded Opus decoder. Physical Bluetooth AV offset and microphone routing require separate evidence under #21/#22.

Host verification uses the RUNBOOK JDK 17/SDK 35 build command, including all APKs, Receiver test APK assembly, the three JVM suites and all four lint tasks. Regression fixtures cover revision/header compatibility, retired audio rejection, malformed sizes, video PTS scheduling, late drops, buffering, video-only session readiness, decoder failure, geometry replacement, reconnect and repeated Stop. Codec doubles establish host logic and resource-release behavior; they do not decode real H.264 or establish Glasses hardware stability.

Physical checks require a Manager-recorded reservation for both devices and their exact debugging route:

1. Install and hash both updated endpoints and the test APK, preserving existing experimental applications/private state. Bootstrap through the authorized Phone path and record source/artifact/config identities.
2. With Bluetooth disconnected, connect and launch an owned changing-video fixture on the actual new virtual display. Check changing packets, render progress, actual Glasses output and a visible native-control effect. Collect receiver and host diagnostics.
3. Repeat with Bluetooth connected, then disconnect its audio route during changing video. Confirm video and controls continue. These observations do not establish AV synchronization or microphone routing.
4. Exercise a current geometry change, Stop/Disconnect and reconnect. Relaunch the fixture on the fresh display, verify fresh timing/progress and inspect actual socket, child, display and codec teardown.

The device driver no longer demands Opus packets. Its audio-focus case checks continued rendered video after independent system focus acquisition. Its volume case changes Receiver system music volume only; it does not prove the Phone's active Bluetooth route. Bluetooth transitions and optical output still need direct observation. Device and human evidence must be reported separately from the host checks.

## Static capture and explicit failures

The pinned encoder requests repeats after 100 ms, but this does not promise indefinite periodic video. Android 15's buffer-source implementation limits repeats to ten per fresh source buffer: [header](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/av/+/refs/tags/android-15.0.0_r1/media/module/bqhelper/include/media/stagefright/bqhelper/GraphicBufferSource.h), [repeat scheduling](https://android.googlesource.com/platform/frameworks/av/+/refs/tags/android-15.0.0_r1/media/module/bqhelper/GraphicBufferSource.cpp). Healthy static reading can therefore become packet-silent. This is platform source evidence; the physical devices' vendor cadence remains unmeasured.

`VideoSilenceTest` exercises production Receiver startup, mutually authenticated loopback TLS, its real scheduler and codec doubles. A rendered static frame remains usable through 6.5 seconds of control-only traffic. Separate cases verify that an explicit END, authenticated transport EOF and malformed framing after the first render close the session, reject later navigation and release the codec. These checks cover observable failures and preserve healthy static behavior. PONG confirms the bridge's control path; it cannot prove that the capture producer is healthy. The pinned stock transport does not distinguish a living silent encoder hang from healthy static capture. No silence-only failure deadline or periodic encoder reset is added.

The host video loop closes in `finally` on upstream EOF, I/O or parsing failure; its existing tests cover failure-reason classification. Normal child exit is expected to close its Unix sockets and reach that path. Actual child-exit/socket propagation and device teardown remain physical checks, rather than claims established by those JVM tests. A producer-loop heartbeat would require a separate qualified backend/contract change; a bridge timer or process-alive flag would not supply equivalent media-health evidence.

## Decoder input backpressure

Issue #21 reproduces a concrete failure mechanism with a finite-capacity codec double: the output worker holds a decoded frame for its valid 335 ms presentation target, exhausting input capacity, and the former single 50 ms input dequeue terminates the session. Input now retries `INFO_TRY_AGAIN_LATER` when output acquisition, release or render callbacks advanced during the poll, or when the output worker is waiting for an already-admitted future presentation. A poll with neither condition still fails. No encoded queue or periodic reset is introduced. The same regression passes after the correction; this establishes a host mechanism, not the cause of the retained historical hardware failure.

Receiver `status.json` includes `decoderProgress`: input/config admission counts, output acquisition/release and callback counts, their last PTS and local monotonic timestamps, input retry count and longest admission wait, pending decode/render counts, and the active presentation target. The closing session retains a snapshot from before codec release clears tracking. Render callbacks remain callback evidence; they do not measure optical presentation. A codec exception, Surface release exception, existing backlog failure or timing failure still closes the session. Host tests cover those failures, rejected later controls and a fresh decoder with a new timing origin.

For a silent changing-content run, launch `.FixtureActivity` on the actual session display and use the existing `stream` instrumentation operation for 660 seconds. Inject an on-Glasses synthetic tap near the end and correlate the Phone fixture's tap counter with Receiver control counters. For a separate static/no-input run, launch that fixture with `--ez staticContent true` and use `operation=static-content`, `seconds=660`, `waitForGo=true`, with no injected tap. That mode draws once and invalidates only on an actual view/input event; its private `fixture-status.json` records static mode, draw count and taps. Static instrumentation requires the initial rendered stream to survive the interval without demanding further encoded or rendered frames. Compare source and Receiver samples, controls and power observations separately. The finite measurement ends with instrumentation cleanup; it does not add a session lifetime limit. Exact installed artifacts, actual hardware codec behavior, Surface teardown/reconnect and optical/native-input observations still need a Manager-assigned device window.
